Our take — train for the spine, wheels for the fringes

For this string of train-linked coastal towns with walkable centres, the train wins on cost, charm and stress, and Brittany's TER network is genuinely good. A car mainly earns its keep at the day-trip edges. The sweet spot for most groups: ride the rails between towns and rent a car for just 1–2 days (or join local tours) to reach Mont-Saint-Michel, Cap Fréhel, the Pointe du Raz and the Carnac stones. Going all-car instead? Budget about €470 more and plan to park outside the walls.

06

Before You Go

Book in this order

① ICE Erfurt → Paris on bahn.de · ② TGV Paris → Rennes on sncf-connect.com · ③ Apartments on Booking, Airbnb, or Gîtes de France. Two to three months ahead halves your fares.

Apartments over hotels

For four people, a two-bedroom apartment with kitchen is cheaper and more comfortable than two hotel rooms. Saint-Malo intra-muros and Quiberon book up first — start there.

One pass, whole group

The BreizhGo 7-day pass is €110 — and it covers up to 5 people. Buy two passes (€220 total) on the BreizhGo app the day before each one activates.

Pack for changeable weather

Summer highs of 20–23 °C, but Brittany weather turns fast. Bring a light rain jacket, layers, and proper shoes for the GR34 coastal path.

Reserve dinner the day before

Small Breton restaurants have 6–10 tables. A group of four needs to call ahead in summer — your apartment host can usually help.

Tide times matter

At Saint-Malo and Mont-Saint-Michel, the tide governs what's possible. Check horaire-marée.fr before either day. A spring tide turns the ramparts walk into a spectacle.
